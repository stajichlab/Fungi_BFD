8. Training versus evidence: what RNA-seq contributes

RNA-seq helps funannotate most as evidence for the final gene models, not as a source of ab initio training genes. The companion notes are in evidence_and_alignment_methods.md in the same BFD directory. Each point below says whether it was measured.

  1. Training from RNA-seq ORFs is rarely better than training from BUSCO genes by more than about 1 point. Measured; 4 genomes, experiment A final (Section 10).
    • At 100 complete PASA training models and below, BUSCO training gives 0.9-4.6 points higher holdout locus F1 in all four genomes.
    • At 500 models and above, the difference is between −1.0 and +1.7 points. PASA training is ahead in B. cinerea and C. neoformans.
    • On S. commune (93 complete models), BUSCO training beats the previous production path by +8.2 locus Sn / +5.0 Pr, and PASA with R1 + R2 by +8.6 / +10.0 (Table M6).
    • Most of BUSCO's edge came from single-exon genes, which PASA training sets lacked until single-exon training was added (Tables M4, M5).
  2. RNA-seq's main value is as evidence, mainly through PASA models in EVM. Measured.
    • On N. crassa, the alignment fixes R1 + R2 changed holdout locus Sn / Pr by +2.0 / +1.8 when both the training set and the EVM evidence changed (txR1R2 own minus tx own). Training alone gave only +0.3 / +0.4 (txR1R2 fixed minus tx fixed). Evidence alone, within txR1R2, gave +1.2 / +0.6 (own minus fixed); by difference, about +1.7 / +1.4, if the effects add (Table M5).
    • B. cinerea shows the same pattern: +0.7 / +0.5 combined (txR1 own minus tx own), −0.2 / −0.3 from training alone, and +0.5 / 0.0 from evidence alone.
    • EVM weights explain this: PASA models carry weight 6, against 1 for Augustus, GeneMark, SNAP, proteins and transcripts, and 2 for Augustus HiQ (the EVM Weights line in funannotate-predict.log).
  3. RNA-seq alignments also give Augustus its exon and intron hints. Not measured separately. No hints-on against hints-off arm was run, so the share of the gain that comes through hints is unknown.
  4. GeneMark did not use RNA-seq in these experiments. Measured (configuration). The arms ran GeneMark-ES, which is ab initio, from a precomputed genemark.genome.gtf. GeneMark-ET or EP with RNA-seq or protein hints was not tested.
  5. Practical conclusion. Keep RNA-seq in every run, for evidence. Train from PASA only when there are enough complete models, which is what the gate controls. BUSCO training is a safe default below that threshold.

9. Transcript alignment and PASA evidence quality

A conversion error in funannotate, present since 2018, broke most spliced minimap2 transcript alignments before PASA saw them. Fixing it recovered the spliced evidence.

  • The error (library.bam2gff3 and bam2ExonsHints; logic from 2018, commits a9ed8f2, bc661b4, b77692b):
    • substitutions and deletions in the minimap2 cs tag did not advance the genome position, so later exon boundaries shifted;
    • soft-clipped bases counted as aligned;
    • intron motifs were checked against the SAM flag, which dropped reverse-oriented spliced contigs from unstranded libraries.
  • The fix takes coordinates from the CIGAR, excludes clips from the Target range, accepts consensus introns in either orientation, and uses the alignment strand for PASA and the motif strand for EVM and hints. PR nextgenusfs/funannotate#1210; funannotate v1.9.0-rc.3.

  • Most minimap2 introns that do not match RefSeq lie inside RefSeq genes but differ from the annotated intron (6.3% of introns), or lie outside annotated genes (3.0%). These are likely isoforms or unannotated loci, not alignment errors. Exonerate polishing is therefore not needed.
  • gmap as PASA's own aligner added nothing over blat and took 67% longer. gmap alone gave the weakest training set.
  • gmap (versions 2021-12-17, 2023-04-28 and 2025-07-31) segfaults on some tandem-repeat contigs. PASA v2.6.1-rc.2 retries in chunks and skips those transcripts. The root cause was traced to Pairpool_clean_join and patched outside the image.
  • Relaxing PASA validation (identity 90%, no splice-boundary rule) raised valid spliced alignments from 64% to 88% and exact training chains by 26%. It did not clearly improve predictions: txRel own 65.8 / 72.9 against txR1 own 65.4 / 73.1, that is +0.4 Sn / −0.2 Pr (Table M5). Defaults were therefore chosen from prediction-level scores.
  • F1 in production: CDS lengths not divisible by 3 had a median share of about 0.45 in PASA sets dated 2026-07-06 to 09-10. C. neoformans H99 went from 160 complete models in production to 2,002 of 2,704 after the fix.
  • Production RNA-seq identity (8,007 PASA-trained genomes, transcript level): 69% at ≥ 99%, 31% at 90-99%, 0.6% below 90%. Transcript identity reads 0.4-0.9 points below read identity, so about 20-31% of genomes have divergent reads.

10. Calibrating the complete-model gate (experiment A, final)

BUSCO training beats PASA training at 100 complete models and below in all four genomes. At 500 and above the difference is between −1.0 and +1.7 points. Under the conservative rule the per-genome threshold is 300 to 2,000, or not reached (N. crassa). The funannotate default is still 500; experiment B decides whether it changes.

  • Design. Within each genome, the complete PASA training models were subsampled to N = 50-2,000, with 5 draws for N ≤ 500 and 3 above. Each subset trained Augustus and SNAP, and was scored on the holdout chromosomes with the same fixed evidence as BUSCO training.
  • Rule (conservative). The threshold is the smallest N where the lower 95% bound of (PASA-trained − BUSCO-trained) holdout locus F1 is at least 0.
